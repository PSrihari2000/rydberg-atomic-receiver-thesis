\section{The three receiver types --- overview}

The paper compares \textbf{three} receiver architectures throughout. Every
figure and every equation belongs to one (or a comparison) of these:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Conventional RF receiver}: a classical dipole-antenna +
  LNA superheterodyne receiver. Used only as a performance baseline
  (Fig.~2 bottom row, Fig.~8, Fig.~9, Fig.~10(a)). It has no Rydberg
  physics at all --- just antenna gain, LNA gain, and thermal noise.
  \item \textbf{LO-free Rydberg atomic receiver}: reads the RF field by
  directly observing the Autler--Townes (AT) splitting of the EIT peak.
  No extra RF tone is injected; the receiver only measures the
  \emph{amplitude} of the incident field via the splitting interval.
  Simple to build, but coarse (one scalar Rabi-frequency estimate per
  scan window) and only measures amplitude, not phase.
  \item \textbf{LO-dressed Rydberg atomic receiver}: an additional
  strong ``local oscillator'' (LO) RF tone is deliberately injected
  on-resonance with the same Rydberg transition, turning the atoms into
  a coherent RF mixer/heterodyne receiver. It measures \emph{both}
  amplitude and phase, and (per the paper's own numerical results) is
  the most sensitive of the three.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Section II: Preliminaries --- EIT, the master equation, Doppler}

\subsection{Autler--Townes splitting readout (Eq.~1)}
\begin{equation}
\Delta\nu =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\Omega_\text{RF}}{2\pi}, & \text{scanning the coupling laser},\\[2mm]
\dfrac{\Omega_\text{RF}}{2\pi}\dfrac{f_p}{f_c}, & \text{scanning the probe laser},
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\end{equation}
\textbf{What it means:} $\Delta\nu$ is the frequency separation you
actually observe between the two split EIT peaks. $\Omega_\text{RF}$ is
the RF Rabi frequency you are trying to measure; $f_p,f_c$ are the probe
and coupling laser frequencies. \textbf{Significance:} this is the
foundational ``readout equation'' of the LO-free receiver --- it says the
splitting you see on the photodetector directly encodes $\Omega_\text{RF}$,
which is why scanning the coupling laser (giving the clean $\Delta\nu=\Omega_\text{RF}/2\pi$
form) is preferred.

\subsection{Beer--Lambert probe transmission (Eq.~2--4)}
\begin{equation}
P_\text{out}(t) = P_\text{in}\exp\{-k_pL\,\Im(\chi(t))\}, \tag{2}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
P_\text{in} = \frac{\pi}{2Z_0}\left(\frac{d\,\Omega_p\hbar}{2\sqrt{\wp_{12}}}\right)^2, \tag{3}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\chi(t) = C_0\,\rho_{21}(t), \qquad C_0 = \frac{-2N_0\wp_{12}}{\epsilon_0\hbar\Omega_p}. \tag{4}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Terms:} $P_\text{in}$ = incident probe laser intensity;
$P_\text{out}(t)$ = transmitted probe intensity measured on the
photodetector (PD) --- \emph{this is the quantity every $P_\text{out}$
figure in the paper plots}; $k_p=2\pi/\lambda_p$ = probe wavenumber; $L$
= vapor-cell length; $\chi(t)$ = complex susceptibility of the medium;
$\rho_{21}(t)$ = the $|1\rangle$--$|2\rangle$ coherence (off-diagonal
density-matrix element) --- \emph{the single quantity every other
formula in the paper ultimately reduces to}; $N_0$ = total atomic
number density; $\wp_{12}$ = dipole moment of the $|1\rangle$--$|2\rangle$
transition; $d$ = probe beam $1/e^2$ diameter; $\Omega_p$ = probe Rabi
frequency; $Z_0$ = free-space impedance; $\epsilon_0$ = vacuum
permittivity.
\textbf{Significance:} Eq.~2 is literally the Beer--Lambert absorption
law with the absorption coefficient written in terms of the imaginary
part of the atomic susceptibility --- \emph{this is the master formula
used to generate every single $P_\text{out}$ curve/surface in the whole
paper (Fig.~3, 4, 5, 6)}. Eq.~3 tells you how $P_\text{in}$ and
$\Omega_p$ are tied together through the beam diameter $d$ --- this is
the equation whose self-consistency with the stated $P_\text{in}=20.7\,\mu$W
and $d=0.76$~mm is the known internal inconsistency in the paper (using
Eq.~3 with $d=0.76$~mm and $\Omega_p/2\pi=8$~MHz gives
$P_\text{in}=39.08\,\mu$W, not 20.7~$\mu$W).

\subsection{Master equation and Hamiltonian (Eq.~5--7)}
\begin{equation}
\dot{\boldsymbol\rho} = \frac{\partial\boldsymbol\rho}{\partial t} = -\frac{j}{\hbar}[\mathbf H,\boldsymbol\rho] + \mathcal L. \tag{5}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf H = \frac{\hbar}{2}
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & \Omega_p & 0 & 0\\
\Omega_p & -2\Delta_p & \Omega_c & 0\\
0 & \Omega_c & -2(\Delta_p+\Delta_c) & \Omega\\
0 & 0 & \Omega & -2(\Delta_p+\Delta_c+\Delta_\text{RF})
\end{bmatrix}. \tag{6}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Terms:} 4-level ladder system $|1\rangle\!\to\!|2\rangle\!\to\!|3\rangle\!\to\!|4\rangle$
(Cs: $6S_{1/2}\to 6P_{3/2}\to 47D_{5/2}\to 48P_{3/2}$). $\Omega_p,\Omega_c$
= probe/coupling Rabi frequencies (fixed, always present). $\Omega$ (the
bare, unsubscripted symbol in row/col 3--4) is the $|3\rangle$--$|4\rangle$
coupling --- \textbf{the paper explicitly states this ``differs for
LO-free and LO-dressed receivers''}: for the LO-free receiver
$\Omega=\Omega_\text{RF}$ (confirmed by its use in Eq.~8, Sec.~III-A);
for the LO-dressed receiver $\Omega=\Omega_\text{total}$ (Eq.~26/28, a
complex-phasor combination of the LO and RF fields). $\Delta_p,\Delta_c,\Delta_\text{RF}$
= probe/coupling/RF detunings. \textbf{Significance:} this is \emph{the}
Hamiltonian used for every QuTiP steady-state solve in the whole
project (Fig.~3--7 all reuse this exact 4-level structure, only
changing which detunings are swept and what goes in the $\Omega$ slot).

\begin{equation}
\mathcal L =
\begin{bmatrix}
\gamma_2\rho_{22} & -\gamma_{12}\rho_{12} & -\gamma_{13}\rho_{13} & -\gamma_{14}\rho_{14}\\
-\gamma_{21}\rho_{21} & \gamma_3\rho_{33}-\gamma_2\rho_{22} & -\gamma_{23}\rho_{23} & -\gamma_{24}\rho_{24}\\
-\gamma_{31}\rho_{31} & -\gamma_{32}\rho_{32} & \gamma_4\rho_{44}-\gamma_3\rho_{33} & -\gamma_{34}\rho_{34}\\
-\gamma_{41}\rho_{41} & -\gamma_{42}\rho_{42} & -\gamma_{43}\rho_{43} & -\gamma_4\rho_{44}
\end{bmatrix},\quad \gamma_{ij}=\frac{\gamma_i+\gamma_j}{2}. \tag{7}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Terms:} $\gamma_2,\gamma_3,\gamma_4$ = spontaneous-emission
(population decay) rates of levels 2,3,4 ($\gamma_1=0$, ground state).
$\gamma_{ij}$ = coherence dephasing rate between levels $i,j$.
\textbf{Significance:} this is a standard \emph{cascade decay} Lindblad
superoperator --- equivalent to QuTiP collapse operators
$\sqrt{\gamma_2}|1\rangle\langle2|,\ \sqrt{\gamma_3}|2\rangle\langle3|,\ \sqrt{\gamma_4}|3\rangle\langle4|$.
This is exactly what should be passed to \texttt{qutip.steadystate()}.

\subsection{Closed-form steady state (Eq.~8) --- weak-probe approximation}
\begin{equation}
\rho_{21} = \cfrac{-j(\Omega_p/2)}{\gamma_{21}-j\Delta_p-\cfrac{(\Omega_c/2)^2}{\gamma_{31}-j(\Delta_p+\Delta_c)-\cfrac{(\Omega_\text{RF}/2)^2}{\gamma_{41}-j(\Delta_p+\Delta_c+\Delta_\text{RF})}}}. \tag{8}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Significance:} this is the paper's own \emph{analytic}
(non-QuTiP) steady-state solution, derived in Appendix~A under the
weak-probe assumption $\rho_{11}\approx1$. \textbf{Caution (verified
this session):} this formula is only well-behaved for small
$\Omega_\text{RF}$ --- because $\gamma_4/2\pi=1.7$~kHz is so tiny, the
nested term $(\Omega_\text{RF}/2)^2/\gamma_{41}$ blows up almost
immediately, making Eq.~8 give unphysical results (gain $>1$, or an
instant collapse to zero) once $\Omega_\text{RF}$ is more than a
fraction of a kHz. \textbf{Do not use Eq.~8 to generate Fig.~3--type
data} --- the paper itself only uses full QuTiP for those figures; Eq.~8
is presented for physical intuition/derivation purposes only.

\subsection{Doppler broadening (Eq.~9--11)}
\begin{equation}
g(v_z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_v}e^{-v_z^2/(2\sigma_v^2)}, \tag{9}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Delta_p(v_z)=\Delta_p-\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_p}v_z,\qquad
\Delta_c(v_z)=\Delta_c+\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_c}v_z, \tag{10}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\bar\rho_{21}(\Delta_p)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\rho_{21}(v_z)\,g(v_z)\,dv_z. \tag{11}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Terms:} $v_z$ = atomic velocity along the beam axis;
$\sigma_v=\sqrt{k_BT/m_\text{Rb}}$ = thermal velocity spread; $g(v_z)$ =
Maxwell--Boltzmann velocity distribution. \textbf{Significance:} this
models thermal (room-temperature vapor) Doppler broadening from
counter-propagating probe/coupling beams. \textbf{Important
project-specific finding:} this project deliberately does \emph{not}
use Eq.~9--11 for Fig.~3, because (a) the text never applies it near the
actual Sec.~V-A simulation parameters, and (b) a real convergence test
showed the AT splitting is completely erased once Doppler averaging is
properly converged --- inconsistent with the real published Fig.~3.
The paper's own cited source for the cold-atom EIT-AT spectrum
explicitly uses a cold-atom (no-Doppler) model for exactly this kind of
plot.

\section{Section III-A: LO-free Rydberg atomic receiver model}

\subsection{RF Rabi frequency definition (Eq.~12)}
\begin{equation}
\Omega_\text{RF} = \frac{|E_\text{RF}|\wp_\text{RF}}{\hbar}. \tag{12}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Terms:} $E_\text{RF}$ = incident RF electric field amplitude;
$\wp_\text{RF}$ = dipole moment of the $|3\rangle$--$|4\rangle$
(Rydberg--Rydberg) transition ($\wp_\text{RF}=-1443.459\,ea_0$, paper-stated).
\textbf{Significance:} this is the fundamental
field$\to$Rabi-frequency conversion used everywhere a distance or
transmit power needs to be turned into $\Omega_\text{RF}$ (Fig.~5, Fig.~7).

\subsection{Signal model and link budget (Eq.~13--18)}
\begin{align}
y &= \sqrt{P_\text{Rx}}\,hx+n_\text{ex}, \qquad P_\text{Rx}=\frac{|E_\text{RF}|^2}{2Z_0}, \tag{13}\\
P_\text{Rx} &= P_\text{Tx}G_\text{Tx}G_\text{Rx}\left(\frac{\lambda}{4\pi d_\text{Tx-Rx}}\right)^2 \quad\text{(Friis, classical antennas)}, \tag{14}\\
S_\text{Rx} &= \frac{P_\text{Tx}G_\text{Tx}}{4\pi d_\text{Tx-Rx}^2} \quad\text{(power flux density at the Rydberg receiver)}, \tag{15}\\
P_\text{Rx} &= S_\text{Rx}A_\text{eff} \quad\text{(Rydberg receiver has no metallic antenna/gain, only an effective aperture)}, \tag{16}\\
A_\text{eff} &= \frac{2Z_0N_\text{atoms}\wp_\text{RF}^2\omega_\text{RF}}{\hbar\Gamma_\text{FWHM}}, \qquad \omega_\text{RF}=2\pi f_\text{RF}, \tag{17}\\
z &\triangleq \Omega_\text{RF}[k] = \frac{\wp_\text{RF}}{\hbar}|E_\text{RF}hx+n_\text{Ry}|. \tag{18}
\end{align}
\textbf{Terms:} $P_\text{Tx},G_\text{Tx}$ = Tx power/antenna gain;
$G_\text{Rx}$ = classical Rx antenna gain (\emph{does not apply} to the
Rydberg receiver, which has no metallic antenna --- Eq.~14 is quoted
only for the conventional-receiver comparison); $d_\text{Tx-Rx}$ = link
distance; $N_\text{atoms}$ = number of atoms effectively contributing
(\textbf{note: the paper never gives a numeric value or formula for
$N_\text{atoms}$ anywhere} --- this is a genuine, confirmed gap, not an
oversight on the reader's part); $\Gamma_\text{FWHM}$ = the ``intrinsic
EIT linewidth'' (also never given a single unambiguous closed-form
value usable across all figures --- see the per-figure notes below).
\textbf{Significance:} this whole chain (Eq.~14$\to$15$\to$16$\to$17$\to$12)
is exactly how a physical link distance becomes an $\Omega_\text{RF}$
value to feed into the quantum-optical model --- it is the backbone of
Fig.~5 and Fig.~7's distance/power axes. $A_\text{eff}$ (Eq.~17) is the
single most important LO-free link-budget quantity: notice it scales
with $N_\text{atoms}$ and the transition properties, \emph{not} the
physical cross-sectional area of the vapor cell (explicitly stated,
Appendix~B) --- a deliberate departure from classical antenna theory.

\subsection{Discrete-time observation model (Eq.~19--23)}
\begin{align}
x(t) &\approx x[k],\ t\in[kT_m,(k+1)T_m], \tag{19}\\
\Delta_c(t) &= \Delta_{c,\min}+\frac{\Delta_{c,\max}-\Delta_{c,\min}}{T_m}(t-kT_m), \tag{20}\\
v_k(t) &= R_\text{PD}P_\text{out}(\Delta_c(t))+n_{\text{Ry},k}(t), \tag{21}\\
v_k[m] &= R_\text{PD}P_\text{out}(\Delta_c[m])+n_{\text{Ry},k}[m], \tag{22}\\
z[k] &\triangleq \Omega_\text{RF}[k] = \mathcal E\left(v_k[m]_{m=0}^{M-1}\right). \tag{23}
\end{align}
\textbf{Significance:} formalizes the LO-free receiver as a
``scan-and-estimate'' architecture --- the coupling laser is swept
across a detuning window once per measurement window $T_m$, the PD
trace is sampled at $M$ points, and an AT-splitting extraction
procedure $\mathcal E(\cdot)$ (peak-separation detection or model-based
fitting) turns the whole trace into a single scalar estimate of
$\Omega_\text{RF}$. This directly explains why the LO-free receiver's
symbol rate is capped at $R_s\le1/T_m$ --- it fundamentally needs a
whole scan to produce one number.

\section{Section III-B: LO-dressed Rydberg atomic receiver model}

\subsection{Field superposition and envelope detection (Eq.~24--26)}
\begin{align}
E_\text{LO} &= A_\text{LO}\cos(2\pi f_\text{LO}t+\phi_\text{LO}), \tag{24a}\\
E_\text{RF} &= A_\text{RF}\cos(2\pi f_\text{RF}t+\phi_\text{RF}), \tag{24b}\\
E_\text{total} &= E_\text{LO}+E_\text{RF} \notag\\
&=\sqrt{A_\text{LO}^2+A_\text{RF}^2+2A_\text{LO}A_\text{RF}\cos(2\pi\Delta f\,t+\Delta\phi)}\cos(2\pi f_\text{LO}t+\phi_\text{LO}), \tag{25}\\
E_\text{total} &\approx [A_\text{LO}+A_\text{RF}\cos(2\pi\Delta f\,t+\Delta\phi)]\cos(2\pi f_\text{LO}t+\phi_\text{LO}) \quad (A_\text{RF}/A_\text{LO}\ll1). \tag{26}
\end{align}
\textbf{Terms:} $\Delta f=f_\text{LO}-f_\text{RF}$, $\Delta\phi=\phi_\text{LO}-\phi_\text{RF}$.
\textbf{Significance:} the LO and RF fields beat together; Eq.~26 is a
second-order Taylor expansion in the small ratio $A_\text{RF}/A_\text{LO}$
showing the beat envelope is (to good approximation) a slow cosine at
frequency $\Delta f$ riding on top of the fast carrier at $f_\text{LO}$.
This is the physical origin of the LO-dressed receiver's demodulation.

\subsection{Complex Rabi-frequency phasor and closed-form $\rho_{21}$ (Eq.~27)}
The Hamiltonian's coupling becomes
$\Omega_\text{total}=\Omega_\text{LO}+e^{j(2\pi\Delta f\,t+\Delta\phi)}\Omega_\text{RF}$
(a \textbf{complex phasor}, not a plain real cosine --- confirmed and
used exactly this way in this project's Fig.~6 build). Under the
simplifying assumptions $\gamma_3=\gamma_4=0$ (states 3,4 are
long-lived/metastable relative to state 2) and full resonance
$\Delta_p=\Delta_c=\Delta_\text{LO}=0$:
\begin{equation}
\rho_{21}\big|_{(\Delta_p,\Delta_c,\Delta_\text{LO},\gamma_3,\gamma_4)=0}
= \frac{j\gamma_2\Omega_p|\Omega_\text{total}|^2}{\gamma_2^2|\Omega_\text{total}|^2+2\Omega_p^2\left(\Omega_c^2+\Omega_p^2+|\Omega_\text{total}|^2\right)}. \tag{27}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Significance:} this is the LO-dressed analogue of Eq.~8 ---
another closed-form, but this time valid specifically at exact
resonance and with the metastable-state approximation, and it is what
Eq.~28 onward is built from.

\subsection{Practical output and the intrinsic gain coefficient (Eq.~28--30)}
\begin{equation}
P_\text{out}(t) = \bar P_0 + \kappa\Omega_\text{RF}\cos(2\pi\Delta f\,t+\Delta\phi), \tag{28}
\end{equation}
where $\bar P_0=P_\text{in}e^{-\alpha}e^{\alpha\Lambda(\Omega_\text{LO},\Gamma)}$,
$\alpha=k_pLC_0\bar A$, $\bar A=\gamma_2\Omega_p/(\gamma_2^2+2\Omega_p^2)$,
$\kappa=\alpha\bar P_0\kappa_\rho$, $\kappa_\rho=\partial\Lambda(\Omega_\text{LO},\Gamma)/\partial\Omega_\text{LO}$,
$\Gamma=\Omega_p\sqrt{2(\Omega_c^2+\Omega_p^2)/(2\Omega_p^2+\gamma_2^2)}$,
and $\Lambda(a,b)=b^2/(b^2+a^2)$ (a normalized Lorentzian, HWHM $=b$,
FWHM $=2b$).
\begin{equation}
P_\text{readout}(t)=P_\text{out}(t)-\bar P_0 = |P(\Delta f)|\cos(2\pi\Delta f\,t+\Delta\phi), \tag{29}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Omega_\text{RF} = \frac{|P(\Delta f)|}{|\kappa|}. \tag{30}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Terms:} $\bar P_0$ = average (DC) probe transmission set purely
by the LO bias $\Omega_\text{LO}$; $\kappa$ = the \textbf{intrinsic gain
coefficient} --- the single most important LO-dressed design
parameter, since the whole point of dressing with an LO is to maximize
$|\kappa|$; $|P(\Delta f)|$ = amplitude of the single-sided Fourier
spectrum of $P_\text{out}(t)$ at $\Delta f$, i.e., what you actually
measure with a spectrum analyzer/lock-in. \textbf{Significance:} Eq.~28
says $P_\text{out}(t)$ oscillates \emph{linearly} with $\Omega_\text{RF}$
around a bias set by $\Omega_\text{LO}$ --- exactly the ``linearized
sensor'' behavior a heterodyne receiver needs. Eq.~30 is the actual
measurement recipe: measure the Fourier peak at $\Delta f$, divide by
the known $\kappa$, and you have $\Omega_\text{RF}$ (and hence, via
Eq.~12, the field amplitude). \textbf{Note carefully:} $\Gamma$ here is
a \emph{different} quantity from the ``$\Gamma_\text{FWHM}$'' used in the
LO-free Eq.~17/48 --- see the Fig.~4-vs-Fig.~6 linewidth-convention
warning below.

\subsection{Noise chain, NEF, QPN/PSN/TN (Eq.~31--47, Fig.~2)}
Fig.~2 gives the explicit block diagram (Tx $\to$ RF signal $\to$ vapor
cell $\to$ PD $\to$ LNA $\to$ LIA $\to$ observation) for all three
receivers side by side, annotating exactly which noise term
(extrinsic/QPN/PSN/TN) enters at which stage and with what
multiplicative gain factor. Key formulas:
\begin{align}
I_\text{AC} &= D\left(|\kappa|\frac{\wp_\text{RF}}{\hbar}\sqrt{P_\text{Rx}}+D|\kappa|\frac{\wp_\text{RF}}{\hbar}n_\text{ex}+Dn_\text{PSN}\right), \tag{31}\\
P_\text{eAC} &= I_\text{AC}^2R_L, \tag{32}\\
P_\text{LNA} &= G_\text{LNA}P_\text{eAC}+\sigma_\text{TN}^2, \tag{33}\\
z^\text{LO} &= \sqrt{P_\text{Rx}G_\text{LNA}R_L}\,D|\kappa|\frac{\wp_\text{RF}}{\hbar}hx+n_{\text{Ry,LO}}. \tag{34}
\end{align}
\begin{equation}
\text{NEF}=\frac{|E_\text{RF}|_\text{min}}{\sqrt{B}} \tag{35}
\end{equation}
(bandwidth-normalized noise-floor field --- smaller is better).
\begin{align}
\text{NEF}_\text{ex} &= \sqrt{\frac{16\pi f_\text{RF}^2}{3\epsilon_0c^3}\Theta(f_\text{RF},T)}, \tag{36}\\
\Theta(f_\text{RF},T) &= \hbar f_\text{RF}
\begin{cases}
\tfrac12 n_\text{th}(f_\text{RF},T)+\tfrac12, & \text{homodyne},\\
2n_\text{th}(f_\text{RF},T)+1, & \text{heterodyne},
\end{cases} \tag{37}\\
n_\text{th}(f_\text{RF},T) &= \frac{1}{\exp(\hbar f_\text{RF}/k_BT)-1}, \tag{38}\\
\sigma_\text{ex}^2 &= \frac{\text{NEF}_\text{ex}^2}{2Z_0}A_\text{eff}B. \tag{39}
\end{align}
\textbf{Significance:} this is the black-body-radiation (BBR) +
vacuum-fluctuation noise floor that \emph{no free-space receiver can
beat}, expressed via a modified Callen--Welton thermal-noise law.
\begin{align}
\frac{E_\text{SQL}}{\sqrt{\text{Hz}}} &= \frac{\hbar}{\wp_\text{RF}\sqrt{N_\text{atoms}T_2}}, \tag{40}\\
\sigma_\text{QPN}^2 &= \left(\frac{E_\text{SQL}}{\sqrt{\text{Hz}}}\right)^2\frac{A_\text{eff}B}{2Z_0}, \tag{41}\\
\bar I &= \frac{\eta_\text{eff}e}{2\pi\hbar f_p}|P_\text{out}|, \tag{42}\\
\sigma_\text{PSN}^2 &= 2eB\bar I, \tag{43}\\
\sigma_\text{TN}^2 &=
\begin{cases}
4k_BTB, & \text{TIA front-end},\\
Fk_BTB, & \text{LNA front-end},
\end{cases} \tag{44}\\
G_\text{Ry} &= \frac{\partial P_\text{out}}{\partial E_\text{RF}}, \tag{45}\\
\sigma_\text{Ry}^2 &= G_\text{Ry}^2D^2G_\text{LNA}^2\sigma_\text{ex}^2+D^2G_\text{LNA}^2\sigma_\text{QPN}^2+G_\text{LNA}^2\sigma_\text{PSN}^2+\sigma_\text{TN}^2, \tag{46}\\
\sigma_\text{Ry,LO}^2 &= \kappa^2D^2G_\text{LNA}^2\sigma_\text{ex}^2+D^2G_\text{LNA}^2\sigma_\text{QPN}^2+G_\text{LNA}^2\sigma_\text{PSN}^2+\sigma_\text{TN}^2. \tag{47}
\end{align}
\textbf{Terms:} $T_2$ = EIT coherence time; $\eta_\text{eff}$ = PD
quantum efficiency; $D$ = PD responsivity (A/W); $F$ = LNA noise
factor; $R_L$ = TIA/output load resistance. \textbf{Significance:}
Eq.~46 vs Eq.~47 is the single most important noise-comparison result
in the paper --- \emph{same four noise terms, same structure}, but the
LO-free version scales the extrinsic noise by $G_\text{Ry}^2$ (a small
number, since the LO-free receiver ``operates on the natural slope of
an EIT doublet'') while the LO-dressed version scales it by $\kappa^2$,
which \textbf{can be 1--2 orders of magnitude larger} --- this
single fact is the quantitative origin of the LO-dressed receiver's SNR
advantage seen in Fig.~8/9/10. The conventional receiver's noise is
$\sigma_\text{Conv}^2=G_\text{LNA}^2\sigma_\text{ex}^2+\sigma_\text{TN}^2$
(Fig.~2 bottom row, page 12) --- notice it has \emph{no} QPN or PSN term
at all, since it has no atomic/optical stage.

\section{Section IV-A: SNR performance}

\subsection{LO-free SNR (Eq.~48--51)}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R \equiv \frac{\Omega_\text{RF}}{\Gamma_\text{FWHM}}, \tag{48}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
G(\mathcal R) = \frac{\mathcal R^2}{1+\frac{1}{2\mathcal R^2}}, \tag{49}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\text{SNR}_\text{Ry} = \frac{P_\text{Rx}|h|^2}{\sigma_\text{Ry}^2}\left(\frac{\wp_\text{RF}}{\hbar\Gamma_\text{FWHM}}\right)^2G(\mathcal R), \tag{50}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
E_\text{min}^\text{strong} = \frac{\hbar\Gamma_\text{EIT}}{\wp_\text{RF}}\left(\frac{2Z_0\sigma_\text{Ry}^2}{A_\text{eff}|h|^2}\right)^{1/4}. \tag{51}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Terms:} $\mathcal R$ = the single dimensionless figure of merit
for LO-free resolvability (RF coupling strength measured in units of
the intrinsic linewidth). $G(\mathcal R)$ = an information-theoretic
penalty (derived from Fisher information in Appendix~D) that punishes
small $\mathcal R$ --- it collapses toward 0 as $\mathcal R\to0$ and
approaches $\mathcal R^2$ for $\mathcal R\gg1$. $E_\text{min}^\text{strong}$
= the receiver's sensitivity floor (minimum detectable field),
obtained by setting $\text{SNR}_\text{Ry}=1$. \textbf{Significance:}
Eq.~48 is exactly the $x$-axis of Fig.~4, and Eq.~49's threshold
behavior (near $\mathcal R=1$) is exactly what Fig.~4's white dashed
lines/yellow $\mathcal R=1$ line illustrate. The paper reports a
computed sensitivity of $38.15\,\mu\text{V/cm}/\sqrt{\text{Hz}}$ from
Eq.~51, cross-checked against the literature.

\subsection{LO-dressed SNR (Eq.~52, 61)}
\begin{equation}
\text{SNR}_\text{Ry,LO} = \frac{G_\text{LNA}R_LD^2\kappa^2\frac{\wp_\text{RF}^2}{\hbar^2}P_\text{Rx}|h|^2}{\sigma_\text{Ry,LO}^2}. \tag{52}
\end{equation}
Restructured, more intuitive form:
\begin{equation}
\text{SNR}_\text{Ry,LO} = \cfrac{R_L\frac{\wp_\text{RF}^2}{\hbar^2}P_\text{Rx}|h|^2}{G_\text{LNA}\sigma_\text{ex}^2+\frac{G_\text{LNA}\sigma_\text{QPN}^2}{\kappa^2}+\frac{G_\text{LNA}\sigma_\text{PSN}^2}{D^2\kappa^2}+\frac{\sigma_\text{TN}^2}{D^2\kappa^2G_\text{LNA}}}. \tag{61}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Significance:} Eq.~61 makes explicit \emph{why} the LO-dressed
receiver wins: the useful signal and the extrinsic (BBR) noise both
scale the same way, so $\kappa$ does not help against extrinsic noise
--- but QPN, PSN, and TN are all \emph{divided} by $\kappa^2$, i.e.,
suppressed. The SNR advantage is therefore concentrated in
non-BBR-limited regimes, not a violation of any fundamental noise floor.

\subsection{Conventional receiver SNR (page 12)}
\begin{equation}
\sigma_\text{Conv}^2 = G_\text{LNA}^2\sigma_\text{ex}^2+\sigma_\text{TN}^2, \qquad
\text{SNR}_\text{Conv} = \frac{G_\text{LNA}P_\text{Rx}|h|^2}{\sigma_\text{Conv}^2}.
\end{equation}
Effective aperture used for the conventional receiver: $\lambda_\text{RF}^2/(4\pi)$
(standard dipole-antenna formula, \emph{not} Eq.~17).

\section{Section IV-B: Distortion effect / linear dynamic range (LDR)}

\subsection{LO-free distortion (Fig.~3, Fig.~4 --- already covered by $\mathcal R$, Eq.~48--49)}
Below $\mathcal R=1$ the two AT peaks merge and the splitting cannot be
read at all --- a hard, qualitative failure mode distinct from a smooth
SNR degradation.

\subsection{LO-dressed distortion --- Taylor expansion of $\Lambda$ (Eq.~53--60)}
\begin{equation}
P_\text{out} = P_\text{in}e^{-\alpha}e^{\alpha\Lambda(\Omega_\text{total},\Gamma)}. \tag{53}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Lambda(\Omega_\text{total},\Gamma) = \Lambda_0+\Lambda_0'\delta\Omega+\frac12\Lambda_0''\delta\Omega^2+\frac16\Lambda_0'''\delta\Omega^3+\dots, \tag{54}
\end{equation}
where $\delta\Omega=\Omega_\text{total}-\Omega_\text{LO}$,
$\Lambda_0'=\dfrac{-2\Gamma^2\Omega_\text{LO}}{(\Gamma^2+\Omega_\text{LO}^2)^2}$,
$\Lambda_0''=\dfrac{-2\Gamma^2(\Gamma^2-3\Omega_\text{LO}^2)}{(\Gamma^2+\Omega_\text{LO}^2)^3}$.
\begin{equation}
P_\text{out} = \bar P_0 + \underbrace{\kappa\Omega_\text{RF}\cos\theta}_{\text{desired}}
+ \underbrace{\tfrac14\alpha\bar P_0\Lambda_0''\Omega_\text{RF}^2(1+\cos2\theta)}_{\text{2nd-order distortion}}
+ \underbrace{\tfrac16\alpha\bar P_0\Lambda_0'''\Omega_\text{RF}^3\cos^3\theta}_{\text{3rd-order distortion}}. \tag{55}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\frac{P(\Delta f)}{P(2\Delta f)} = \frac{\kappa\Omega_\text{RF}}{\tfrac14\alpha\bar P_0\Lambda_0''\Omega_\text{RF}^2} \le \epsilon
\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;
\Omega_\text{RF,max}'' = \frac{4\epsilon|\Lambda_0'|}{|\Lambda_0''|}. \tag{56, 57}
\end{equation}
\textbf{IMPORTANT --- verified this session, corrects an earlier
implementation error in this project:} Eq.~57 \textbf{does include}
$\epsilon$. (An earlier build of Fig.~6 in this project mistakenly
treated Eq.~57 as $\epsilon$-free; that must be corrected.) At the true
optimum $\Omega_\text{LO,opt}=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{3}\Gamma$, $\Lambda_0''=0$
exactly (2nd-order distortion vanishes), so Eq.~57 is not usable there
and the 3rd-order bound governs instead:
\begin{equation}
\Omega_\text{RF,max}''' = \sqrt{\frac{6\epsilon|\Lambda_0'|}{|\Lambda_0'''|}}. \tag{58}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Terms:} $\theta=2\pi\Delta f\,t+\Delta\phi$; $\epsilon$ = total
harmonic distortion (THD) tolerance --- \textbf{the paper explicitly
calls this ``the designer-specified tolerance''; no numeric value is
given anywhere in the paper.} Both Eq.~57 and Eq.~58 are therefore
\emph{families of bounds parametrized by $\epsilon$}, not single fixed
numbers, unless you choose an $\epsilon$ yourself (and label it as your
own choice). \textbf{Significance:} this is the complete derivation
behind Fig.~6's shaded ``linear dynamic range'' band and Fig.~7's
upper/lower $P_\text{Rx}$ envelopes --- the LDR is fundamentally a
THD-based (distortion-budget) definition, not a curve-fit or
visual-straightness criterion.

\section{Appendices --- key derivations}

\subsection{Appendix A --- solving the master equation (Eq.~62--67)}
Shows the direct route from the commutator $[\mathbf H,\boldsymbol\rho]_{21}$
(Eq.~62) through the coupled steady-state equations for
$\rho_{21},\rho_{31},\rho_{41}$ (Eq.~63--66) to the closed form in
Eq.~8/67, using the approximations $\rho_{11}\approx1$,
$\rho_{22},\rho_{33},\rho_{44}\approx0$, and $\rho_{32},\rho_{42}\to0$
(``independent-atom'', weak-probe limit).

\subsection{Appendix B --- deriving $A_\text{eff}$ (Eq.~68--74)}
\begin{equation}
\rho_{ee} = \frac{(\Omega_\text{RF}/\Gamma_\text{FWHM})^2}{1+4(\Delta_c/\Gamma_\text{FWHM})^2+2(\Omega_\text{RF}/\Gamma_\text{FWHM})^2}, \qquad \bar R=\Gamma_\text{FWHM}\rho_{ee}. \tag{68,69}
\end{equation}
Near-resonant, weak-limit scaling ($\Delta_c\approx0,\ \Omega_\text{RF}\ll\Gamma_\text{FWHM}$):
\begin{equation}
\bar R = \frac{\Omega_\text{RF}^2}{\Gamma_\text{FWHM}} = \frac{\wp_\text{RF}^2E_\text{RF}^2}{\hbar^2\Gamma_\text{FWHM}}. \tag{70}
\end{equation}
Each absorption--re-emission event removes energy $\hbar\omega_\text{RF}$:
\begin{equation}
P_\text{atom}=\hbar\omega_\text{RF}\bar R=\frac{\wp_\text{RF}^2E_\text{RF}^2\omega_\text{RF}}{\hbar\Gamma_\text{FWHM}},\qquad
P_\text{total}=N_\text{atoms}P_\text{atom}. \tag{71,72}
\end{equation}
Matching to a flux-times-aperture form
$\frac{E_\text{RF}^2}{2Z_0}A_\text{eff}=P_\text{total}$ gives back
exactly Eq.~17/74:
\begin{equation}
A_\text{eff} = \frac{2Z_0N_\text{atoms}\wp_\text{RF}^2\omega_\text{RF}}{\hbar\Gamma_\text{FWHM}}. \tag{74}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Significance:} explicitly confirms $A_\text{eff}$ scales with
$N_\text{atoms}$ and the transition dipole moment/frequency --- ``rather
than the physical cross-section of the vapor cell,'' stated directly in
the text. This is why a geometric fallback for $N_\text{atoms}$ (e.g.,
$N_0\times$beam volume) is explicitly \emph{not} what the authors
intend, even though it's the only practical substitute available.

\subsection{Appendix C --- deriving the LO-dressed optimum (Eq.~75--83)}
\begin{equation}
\Im[\rho_{21}(\Omega_\text{RF})] = \bar A[1-\Lambda(\Omega_\text{LO},\Gamma)-\kappa_\rho\Omega_\text{RF}\cos(2\pi\Delta f\,t+\Delta\phi)]. \tag{75}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_\text{HWHM}^{(3)} = \frac{\Omega_c^2+\Omega_p^2}{2\sqrt{\gamma_2^2+2\Omega_p^2}} \quad\text{(three-level HWHM)}, \tag{77}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Gamma = \frac{2\sqrt2\,\Omega_p}{\sqrt{\Omega_c^2+\Omega_p^2}}\,\Gamma_\text{HWHM}^{(3)} \quad\text{(four-level $\Gamma$, used in Eq.~27--30, 53--58, 79)}. \tag{78}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Omega_\text{LO,opt} = \frac{\sqrt3}{3}\Gamma, \qquad (\kappa_\rho)_\text{max}=\frac{3\sqrt3}{8\Gamma}. \tag{79}
\end{equation}
\textbf{CRITICAL, project-verified distinction:} $\Gamma$ (Eq.~78) is
\textbf{NOT} the same quantity as $\Gamma_\text{FWHM}$ used in Eq.~17/48/50
(the LO-free link-budget linewidth). They are two structurally different
quantities used in two different contexts:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Gamma_\text{FWHM}$ (LO-free, Eq.~48): appears as a normalizer
for $\Omega_\text{RF}$ in the $\Delta_c$-domain (Fig.~4/5 context); the
paper never gives it an unambiguous closed form usable across figures
--- this project settled on a \emph{measured} value from real QuTiP data
(1.2231~MHz) as the standing project convention, after finding the
``obvious'' analytic candidate ($\Gamma_\text{HWHM}^{(3)}$ alone) fails
to reproduce the paper's stated $\mathcal R=1$ threshold behavior.
\item $\Gamma$ (Eq.~78, LO-dressed): the HWHM of the LO-dressed
Lorentzian $\Lambda(\Omega_\text{LO},\Gamma)$ as a function of
$\Omega_\text{LO}$ itself --- purely analytic, $N_0$-independent, and
\emph{does} closely reproduce the paper's stated $\Omega_\text{LO,opt}=4.23$~MHz
when computed from Eq.~78 (this project got 4.2294~MHz).
\end{itemize}
A refined optimum accounting for the $\Omega_\text{LO}$-dependence of
$\bar P_0$ itself (Eq.~80--83):
\begin{equation}
P_\text{out}(t)\approx \bar P_0+\kappa\Omega_\text{RF}\cos(2\pi\Delta f\,t+\Delta\phi), \tag{81}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial\Omega_\text{LO}}=\frac{\partial^2\bar P_0}{\partial\Omega_\text{LO}^2}=0
\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;
\Omega_\text{LO} = \sqrt{\bar A-1+\sqrt{\bar A^2-2\bar A+4}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{3}\Gamma. \tag{82,83}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Significance:} Eq.~83 is a small $\bar A$-dependent correction
to the simple Eq.~79 result --- worth checking numerically against 4.23~MHz
if pursuing extra precision on $\Omega_\text{LO,opt}$.

\subsection{Appendix D --- Fisher information / SNR derivation (Eq.~84--90)}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T(\Delta_c;\Omega_\text{RF}) = 1-\mathcal C\sum_{i=\pm1}\frac{(\Gamma_\text{FWHM}/2)^2}{(\Delta_c-\frac{\Omega_\text{RF}}{2}i)^2+(\Gamma_\text{FWHM}/2)^2}, \qquad \mathcal C\in(0,1). \tag{84}
\end{equation}
\textbf{This is the two-Lorentzian model used for Fig.~5} --- note it
is a \emph{transmission dip} model ($1-\mathcal C\sum\dots$), i.e.,
subtracting two Lorentzian \emph{peaks} from a background of 1, not
adding them --- worth double-checking against how this project's Fig.~5
implementation adds two Lorentzian peaks directly (an equivalent
description when $\mathcal C$ absorbs the sign/normalization, but check
the constant-background piece if revisiting Fig.~5).
\begin{align}
\mathcal I(\Omega_\text{RF}) &= \frac{N_\text{ph}}{2\Gamma_\text{FWHM}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{[\partial_{\mathcal R}\mathcal T]^2}{\mathcal T}\,d\left(\frac{2\Delta_c}{\Gamma_\text{FWHM}}\right), \tag{85}\\
\mathcal I_0(\mathcal R) &\le \mathcal I(\Omega_\text{RF}) \le \frac{1}{1-2\mathcal C}\mathcal I_0(\mathcal R), \qquad \mathcal I_0(\mathcal R)=\frac{8N_\text{ph}\mathcal C^2}{\Gamma_\text{FWHM}^2}\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{2\mathcal R^2}}, \tag{86,87}\\
\mathcal I(\Omega_\text{RF}) &= \frac{8N_\text{ph}\mathcal C^2}{\Gamma_\text{FWHM}^2}\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{2\mathcal R^2}}\beta(\mathcal C), \qquad \beta(\mathcal C)\in\left[1,\tfrac{1}{1-2\mathcal C}\right], \tag{88}\\
\sigma_{\Omega_\text{RF}}^2 &\ge \mathcal I^{-1}(\Omega_\text{RF}) = \frac{\Gamma_\text{FWHM}^2}{8N_\text{ph}\mathcal C^2}\left(1+\frac{1}{2\mathcal R^2}\right), \tag{89}\\
\frac{\Omega_\text{RF}^2}{\sigma_{\Omega_\text{RF}}^2} &= \frac{\mathcal R^2}{1+\frac1{2\mathcal R^2}} \equiv G(\mathcal R). \tag{90}
\end{align}
\textbf{Significance:} this is a rigorous, first-principles
(Poisson-statistics + Cram\'er--Rao) derivation proving $G(\mathcal R)$
is not an ad-hoc fudge factor but the actual information-theoretic
resolvability penalty for estimating $\Omega_\text{RF}$ from a
two-Lorentzian AT-split spectrum. $N_\text{ph}$ = expected photon count
per detection window; $\mathcal C$ = a contrast parameter (paper uses
$\mathcal C\lesssim0.2$ for its weak-probe simulations, giving
$\beta(\mathcal C)\le1.67$, i.e. $\le2.22$~dB of contrast-only slack).
