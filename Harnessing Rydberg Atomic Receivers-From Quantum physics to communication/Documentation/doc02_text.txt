1 Purpose of This Document

This document provides the numerical implementation framework required to extend the ana-
lytical SER­SNR derivations into the distortion regions of Rydberg atomic receivers.

    The previous mathematical document established the analytical operating-region relation-
ships

                                         SNR - SER.                          (1)

    For the conventional receiver this analytical relationship is essentially the complete model.
    For the Rydberg receivers, however, the complete communication chain contains an addi-
tional quantum-response layer:

     SNR - PRx - ERF - RF - atomic response - detected symbol - SER. (2)

    The purpose of this document is not to replace the analytical derivations. Instead, it provides
the numerical procedure required when the atomic response becomes nonlinear or the quantum
measurement becomes ambiguous.

2 Three Receiver Models

The three receiver configurations considered are:
   1. Conventional RF receiver,
   2. LO-free Rydberg atomic receiver,
   3. LO-dressed Rydberg atomic receiver.

2.1 Conventional Receiver

The conventional receiver is modeled as

             y = PRxhx + nConv,                                              (3)

with

             nConv  CN (0, C2 onv).                                          (4)

For Fig. 9,

                                  SNR - SERQAM.                              (5)

No QuTiP simulation is required.

2.2 LO-Free Rydberg Receiver

The LO-free receiver measures RF amplitude through Autler­Townes splitting:

      SNR  PRx  ERF  RF  EIT spectrum  AT peak separation  x^  SER. (6)

    The weak-RF distortion mechanism occurs when the two AT peaks can no longer be reliably
resolved.

    The approximately 5.5 MHz value reported by the reference study is treated only as a
reference point. The actual simulation threshold is determined numerically.

                                                             4
2.3 LO-Dressed Rydberg Receiver

The LO-dressed receiver uses a strong local oscillator:

          SNR  PRx  ERF  RF  Pout  photodetection  x^  SER.  (7)

Inside the linear region,

                           Pout  P¯0 + RF cos(2f t + ).      (8)

    At sufficiently large RF Rabi frequency, this approximation fails and atomic nonlinear dis-
tortion appears.

3 Analytical Region versus Distortion Region

The simulation is divided into an analytical operating region and a numerical distortion region.

3.1 Operating Region

For conventional QAM,

                           SERConv = SERQAM(Conv, M ).       (9)

For LO-free operation,

                           SERLF = SERPAM(LF, M )            (10)

provided

                           |RF|  RF,th.                      (11)

For LO-dressed operation,

                           SERLO = SERQAM(LO, M )            (12)

provided

                           |RF|  RF,max.                     (13)

3.2 Distortion Region

The quantum-response region is obtained numerically:

Master equation  QuTiP  quantum response  lookup table  Monte-Carlo detector  SER.
                                                                                                                       (14)

    QuTiP is therefore used to characterize the atomic receiver response, not to solve a separate
steady-state problem for every Monte-Carlo symbol.

24 Important Simulation Rules

   1. Do not replace the QuTiP response with an arbitrary polynomial.
   2. Do not manually draw the distortion region.
   3. Do not hard-code the 5.5 MHz LO-free threshold without validating the QuTiP response.
   4. Do not use the linear LO-dressed model outside the measured linear dynamic range.
   5. Do not call QuTiP once per Monte-Carlo symbol.
   6. Generate quantum-response lookup tables first.
   7. Use interpolation only inside the simulated response range.
   8. Do not introduce an arbitrary K.
   9. Use the closed-form SNR-to-PRx inversion first.
  10. Convert PRx to ERF and then to RF.
  11. Keep all Rabi frequencies in rad/s internally.
  12. Only convert to MHz for plotting.
  13. Keep physical receiver SNR distinct from the Es/N0 quantity used by analytical Proakis

       equations.
  14. Use identical constellation normalization in analytical and Monte-Carlo SER calculations.
  15. Use sufficiently large Monte-Carlo sample sizes for small SER values.
  16. Document every numerical criterion used to define a distortion boundary.

26 Important Limitation of the Reconstruction

The QuTiP procedure reproduces the methodology of the reference study.
    An exact pixel-for-pixel reproduction of Fig. 9 should not be claimed until all relevant

parameters are matched, including:

   1. quantum-system parameters,
   2. density-matrix convention,
   3. optical-response normalization,
   4. peak-detection rule,
   5. AT-resolution criterion,
   6. LO-dressed linearity criterion,
   7. receiver noise calibration,
   8. SNR definition,
   9. modulation normalization,
  10. Monte-Carlo sample size,
  11. exact parameters used to generate the original figure.

    In particular, the LO-dressed linear dynamic range is a numerical property of the selected
quantum system and operating point.

    Therefore, QuTiP is used to determine the receiver response rather than to manufacture an
analytical distortion equation.
