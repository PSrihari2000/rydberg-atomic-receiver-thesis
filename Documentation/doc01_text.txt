1 Purpose and Overall Mathematical Structure

The objective is to establish the mathematical framework required to reproduce the
Symbol Error Rate (SER) versus Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) curves associated with
Fig. 9 of the reference paper.

    The paper considers three receiver configurations:

   1. Conventional RF receiver,

   2. LO-free Rydberg atomic receiver,

   3. LO-dressed Rydberg atomic receiver.

    The paper states that the SER of PAM is calculated according to Proakis, 4th Edition,
p. 266. The equivalent Proakis expression used here corresponds to Eq. (5-2-45) (with
 = Es/N0); the paper's citation is conventionally written as Eq. (5.246). The QAM SER
is evaluated according to Proakis, p. 278, Eq. (5.279).

    The fundamental communication-theoretic structure is

Receiver model - effective SNR - Proakis SER.                                  (1)

For the Rydberg receivers, an additional receiver-response layer is required:

RF signal - ERF - RF - Rydberg response - effective SNR - SER. (2)
Thus,

                           Conventional: SN R  M -QAM  SER
          LO-free: SN R  ERF  RF  AT condition  M -PAM  SER
LO-dressed: SN R  ERF  RF  linear/nonlinear response  M -QAM  SER

    The classical SER equations are supplied by Proakis. The receiver-specific Rydberg
operating and distortion behavior is supplied by the atomic receiver model and, where
necessary, by the QuTiP quantum-system simulation.

9 Important Interpretation of Fig. 9

9.1 Conventional Curve

The conventional curve is monotonic:

                                SN R  SER  .                       (132)

    It is an ordinary AWGN QAM SER relationship and does not require a quantum-system
simulation for its basic analytical form.

9.2 LO-Free Curve

The LO-free receiver has a low-RF limitation:

PRx  |ERF|  |RF|  AT  .

Eventually the AT peaks become difficult to resolve:

weak RF field - unresolved AT splitting - observation distortion.  (133)

    Therefore a single textbook PAM curve cannot reproduce the entire LO-free distortion
behavior without an additional quantum operating condition.

9.3 LO-Dressed Curve

The LO-dressed receiver has the opposite limitation:

                                   PRx  |ERF|  |RF|  .
Eventually the atomic system leaves its linear dynamic range:

strong RF field - nonlinear atomic response - distortion.          (134)

    Thus the practical LO-dressed SER may decrease initially and deteriorate again when
the RF Rabi frequency becomes too large.
