1 Quantum System and Two-Level Rydberg State


A quantum system is described by a state vector. For the two-level Rydberg-atom model,
the ground state and excited state are denoted by


                                        |g⟩,     |e⟩.


The state of atom i is written as

                          |Φi(t)⟩= ζg,i(t) |g⟩+ ζe,i(t) |e⟩.                           (1)


The quantities ζg,i(t) and ζe,i(t) are the probability amplitudes corresponding to the
ground and excited states, respectively.

The probability of finding the atom in the ground state is

                                         Pg,i(t) = |ζg,i(t)|2 ,

while the probability of finding the atom in the excited state is

                                         Pe,i(t) = |ζe,i(t)|2 .


Since the atom must be in either the ground state or the excited state, the total probability
is unity:
                                        Pg,i(t) + Pe,i(t) = 1.

Therefore,
                                          |ζg,i(t)|2 + |ζe,i(t)|2 = 1.

2 Time-Dependent Schr¨odinger Equation


The time evolution of a quantum state is described by the time-dependent Schr¨odinger
equation.

In the absence of an external interaction,

                        iℏ∂ |Φi(t)⟩= ˆHi |Φi(t)⟩,
                               ∂t

where ˆHi is the free Hamiltonian of the atom. Throughout this document, both i (in iℏ)
and j denote the imaginary unit, i = j = √−1; the letter i used as a subscript is the
vapor-cell index.

When an external electromagnetic field interacts with the atom, an interaction Hamiltonian
ˆVi is introduced. Therefore, the total Hamiltonian is

                                                  ˆHi,tot = ˆHi + ˆVi.


Hence,
                      iℏ∂ |Φi(t)⟩= (ˆHi + ˆVi) |Φi(t)⟩.                          (2)
                            ∂t

The free Hamiltonian describes the internal energy of the atom, whereas ˆVi represents the
interaction between the atom and the incident electromagnetic field.

10  Electric-Dipole Interaction


The electromagnetic field interacts with the electric dipole of the atom.

For an electric dipole moment µ in an electric field E, the interaction energy is represented
by the dipole–field product.

For a charge q located at position r, the electric dipole moment is


                        µ = qr.


Therefore, the interaction energy can be written in terms of the position vector and electric

                             V = qrTE.

11 Two-Level Representation of the Position Operator


In quantum mechanics, the position of the electron is represented by a position operator
rather than an ordinary definite coordinate.

                            ⎡ ˆx⎤
                                                                     ˆr = ⎢⎣ ˆy⎥⎦.
                                                                      ˆz


For the two-level Rydberg-atom system, the state can be expressed as

                          |Φi(t)⟩= ζg,i(t) |g⟩+ ζe,i(t) |e⟩.

                                                                      ˆr = ˆIˆrˆI.

                                           ˆr = (|g⟩⟨g| + |e⟩⟨e|)ˆr (|g⟩⟨g| + |e⟩⟨e|) .

                                            ⟨g| ˆr |g⟩

and
                                              ⟨e| ˆr |e⟩,

                                            ⟨g| ˆr |e⟩

56.5  Relation Between PGD and the CRLB


The PGD estimator incorporates the structural constraint

                       rank(Gk) ≤Lk,    k = 1, . . . , K,                     (465)

whereas the CRLBs in (453) and (463) treat the unfolded channel coefficients as uncon-
strained parameters. Therefore, the CRLB is an accuracy benchmark for the unconstrained
channel-estimation problem; a rank-constrained CRLB would use Lk(I1 +I2 −Lk) complex
degrees of freedom per user instead of I1I2. Both bounds apply to the linearised model
(437) and to unbiased estimators only; the model error from the neglected second-order
term in (387) is set by |(ai1,i2,p + ni1,i2,p)/bi1,i2,p|, i.e. by the reference strength, not by the
SNR. It does not shrink as σ2 →0, so at high SNR it dominates the noise and appears as
an NMSE floor for any estimator based on (437).

The number of real unknowns in an unconstrained complex channel vector is 2KNa, and
the number of real observations is PNa. A generic real-dimension counting condition for
local identifiability of the unconstrained channel is therefore

                   PNa ≥2KNa  =⇒  P ≥2K.                      (466)


This is a generic counting condition rather than a sufficient condition for every pi-
lot/reference configuration. Identifiability also requires D in (460) to have full column
rank. In particular, for K ≤P < 2K, Fgg∗in (451) is invertible, so the Xu-compatible
bound (453) is finite, whereas FR in (462) is singular and the exact bound is infinite. This
is a further reason why (453) is optimistic for short pilot sequences.
